title: "Manipulating Time Containers to Suffer Less"
---

At any moment we feel a "felt width" of the time horizon we are holding in experience. Experience holds an implicitly held or deliberately manipulated span of time, like a container. For instance, right now your felt container is probably something like 30 seconds, or however long you think it will take to read this. Most of the time, the container we hold serves to bring us closer to our immediate goals, whether that be the next minute or the next day. 

Knowing this, we can widen or shrink it on purpose. I can expand it to a whole lifetime, or I can shorten it to one second. When widened, things feel smaller relative to everything else. In my experience, my craving/aversion around things drops when I widen my held time container. For instance, a breakup feels devastating when held within a container of the next day or week, but when held within a container of a lifetime, it feels like one event on a long journey that's not so terrible. The pain is still there, but more affordances arise like fitting the breakup into your story and how you will grow. 

We can also shrink our held time container on purpose to the next second or moment. This helps when things feel too heavy to process. We can choose to shrink to the next breath as a means of interrupting the mental move of gripping around some type of future we really want to see. Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) does the same with a single day: “one day at a time.” 

Suffering seems to be most persistent when we choose to live in the time containers of days to months. This is the range of time where desire feels both vivid and uncertain, so the mind-body system keeps gripping around it. The desired outcome often seems just out of reach, which makes it harder to let go of. A tiny container leaves no future state to contract around. A lifetime container makes any one thing feel small. A container of days to months gives our clinging the environment it needs to survive. In a way, deliberately choosing to shrink or grow our held time container is no different than choosing to deliberately set up our physical environment to facilitate good habits. We are just applying environmental friction to our mind-body system to ensure the process of suffering cannot proliferate.

I find the effects of this similar to what @m_ashcroft teaches through the Alexander Technique (AT). In AT, the basic move is to slow down and expand awareness  to notice and stop the unnecessary "doing" and tension before you act. It develops the skill of noticing unnecessary effort you’re producing and ceasing to do it. All of this plays into the spatial aspect of experience: how expanded or contracted awareness feels. But what I am communicating here is about the temporal aspect of experience: how expanded or contracted your time container feels. Often times both of these things feel coupled, as when our time container grows, so does our awareness, and when our time container shrinks, so does our awareness.
